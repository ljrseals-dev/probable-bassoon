How it works

The room model mirrors a shoebox: early reflections from an image-source calculation and a diffuse tail from an 8-line feedback delay network. The plate is a Dattorro-style network. A one-sample impulse goes in; the stereo response is the impulse response.

Octave-band reverb times come from filtering that response, integrating its energy backward (Schroeder) and timing the drop from -5 to -25 dB, tripled.

Checked and not checked

Checked in headless Chromium: the undamped room matches the Eyring reverb time within 15 percent; damping shortens high bands more than low ones; the exported WAV reads back to the rendered response within 24-bit rounding; a farther source lowers clarity.

Not checked: how it sounds, how a real room compares, or the plate coefficients (typed from memory of Dattorro's design). Clarity and definition follow the usual ISO 3382 definitions but are estimates from one impulse response, not a measurement standard.
